What is interval ear training?

An interval is the distance in pitch between two notes. Interval ear training is the practice of identifying these distances entirely by ear. For example, the distance between C and G is a perfect fifth. The distance between C and E is a major third.

By repeatedly listening to and identifying these intervals, you train your brain to recognize them instantly, forming the basis of relative pitch.

How to practice intervals effectively

The best way to practice is through short, highly focused daily sessions. BananaTone provides a free online tool to drill these intervals efficiently.

  • Start small: Don't try to learn all 12 intervals at once. Pick 2 or 3 distinct intervals.
  • Sing them: When you hear an interval, try to sing the notes back. This connects your ear to your voice.
  • Use song associations: Initially, map intervals to famous songs (e.g., Perfect 4th is "Here Comes the Bride").
  • Be consistent: 5-10 minutes every single day is better than an hour once a week.

Start with these intervals

If you are new to ear training, we recommend starting with these five core intervals:

  • Minor Third (m3): Sounds slightly sad or melancholic. Think of a minor chord or the start of "Greensleeves".
  • Major Third (M3): Sounds bright and happy. Think of the first two notes of "Oh When the Saints".
  • Perfect Fourth (P4): Sounds open and slightly unresolved. Think of "Amazing Grace".
  • Perfect Fifth (P5): Very stable, open, and consonant. Think of "Star Wars" or "Twinkle Twinkle Little Star".
  • Octave (P8): The same note, just higher or lower. Think of "Somewhere Over the Rainbow".
