Relative Pitch Training Online

Relative pitch is the ability to identify or recreate a musical note by comparing it to a reference note. It is the most important skill for playing by ear, transcribing, and improvising.

What is relative pitch?

If someone plays a "C" on a piano and tells you it's a C, and then plays a "G" and you immediately know it's a G because you heard the interval of a perfect fifth, you are using relative pitch.

Instead of memorizing the absolute sound of every note, you learn the distances (intervals) between them and how they relate to a key center.

Relative pitch vs perfect pitch

Perfect pitch (absolute pitch) is the rare ability to identify a note without any reference tone. While it sounds like a superpower, it's very difficult to learn as an adult and isn't strictly necessary to be a great musician.

Relative pitch, on the other hand, can be developed by anyone at any age through consistent training. Almost all professional musicians rely on highly developed relative pitch, not perfect pitch.

The best way to train relative pitch

The secret to developing relative pitch is consistent, daily practice. You should focus on three main areas:

  • Intervals: Learn the distances between two notes.
  • Chords: Learn to recognize major, minor, diminished, and augmented qualities.
  • Functional training: Learn how notes sound relative to a tonal center (e.g., hearing the "3rd" degree of a major scale).

A simple weekly practice routine

To see fast results, practice for 10-20 minutes every day.

  • Days 1-3: Focus on isolated intervals (start with major 2nd, perfect 4th, perfect 5th).
  • Days 4-5: Focus on chord qualities (major vs minor).
  • Days 6-7: Put it together by figuring out simple melodies by ear on your instrument.
