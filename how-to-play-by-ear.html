How to Play by Ear

Playing by ear seems like a magical superpower, but it is actually a mechanical, highly learnable skill. It all comes down to breaking music into recognizable patterns: intervals and chords.

The Myth of playing by ear

Many people believe you must be born with "perfect pitch" to play by ear. This is 100% false. Most professional musicians who play by ear use relative pitch — the ability to hear the distance between notes.

Step 1: Mastering Intervals

Melodies are just a series of distances. If you can hear that the first two notes of a song jump up by a "Perfect 4th", you can play those two notes on any instrument, starting from any key.

Start by using BananaTone's Interval trainer to memorize the sound of the 12 basic distances.

Step 2: Mastering Chord Qualities

The accompaniment of a song is made of chords. You need to instantly recognize if the band is playing a happy (Major) chord or a sad (Minor) chord.

Once your ear can identify the root movement (intervals) and the chord quality (major/minor), you have successfully learned how to play songs by ear.
